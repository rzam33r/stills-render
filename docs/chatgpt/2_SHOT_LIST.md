# 2 — Splitting the script into shots

Each shot is the words spoken while one picture is on screen. The engine
shows the picture on the exact frame its first word is spoken, so what the
shot's words say first is what the picture must show.

## Where to cut

- Start a new shot whenever the image in the listener's head changes: a new
  thing, a new action, a new place, a new time, or a turn ("But…", "And
  yet…", "Here is the twist…").
- Cut at natural phrase breaks: after a comma, before "and", "but", "so",
  "because", or between sentences. Never inside a phrase ("the deadliest |
  water" is wrong).
- 6 to 12 words a shot, about 3 seconds. Up to 16 when the image genuinely
  stays the same. Chapter titles and punchlines may be 2 to 5 words.
- Never change, add or drop a word of the approved script while splitting.

## Picture or graphic

**Picture** (almost every shot): a full painting that shows literally what
the words say at that moment. "If animals avoided a particular pool" → a
deer turning away from a still green pool, the lead character watching. Not
a mood, not a symbol: the thing itself.

**Animated graphic** (about one a minute): a pie, bars, a gauge, a counter,
a road with footprints, a map with a pin, or icons, drawn and animated by
the engine, for a number, a share, a journey, a place or a list the words
say. How to write one: `8_GRAPHICS.md`. Never two in a row, never the first
shot.

White cards and doodles are retired. A chapter title is a picture of the
chapter's first scene with `"paragraph": true` on that shot; a key number
is a graphic.

## Variety

Across a chapter, mix:
- wide establishing scenes (the landscape, the camp, the river),
- the lead character doing the action of the sentence, with a clear emotion,
- close-ups of the key object (a clay pot over a fire, a hand in water),
- comparisons in one picture (left: then, right: now; or split in three),
- small groups of people when the sentence is about "they" or "communities".

Never use the same picture twice in a row. The lead character is in at
least half of the pictures.

## The first and last shots

- Shot 1 is a picture of the cold open's scene, with the lead character.
- The last shot is a picture of the lead character in the viewer's modern
  world.
